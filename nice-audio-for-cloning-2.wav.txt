At 7:03 a.m. on Tuesday, Dr. Eleanor Vale stepped onto Platform 4B with a canvas satchel, two overripe pears, and a note that read, “Meet me at the old clock—no, not that clock.” The station announcer, speaking in a calm but faintly metallic voice, said, “The 07:05 service to St. Ives, via Northbridge, will depart from Platform 4… eventually.” A child asked whether “eventually” meant five minutes or five centuries; his grandmother replied, “Both, if you miss the connection.” Nearby, a cyclist muttered, “Twenty-eight point six kilometres, uphill, in sleet? Brilliant,” while consulting a screen that displayed 28.6 km, 17%, and an icon of a cloud doing something ominous. Eleanor checked her watch twice, then checked it a third time because it was analogue, inherited, and therefore emotionally unreliable. “Bonjour, signora,” called a florist from the concourse. “Your azaleas are here!” “They’re not mine,” Eleanor said. “Nothing with that many vowels is mine.” Then, from the far end of the platform, someone shouted, “Eleanor!” She turned too quickly, dropped a pear, and watched it roll with solemn determination beneath a bench.
